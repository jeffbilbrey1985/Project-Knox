AUTO REPAIR — WHEATON, ILLINOIS

Websites, fleet accounts and service history for auto repair shops in Wheaton, IL.

A retail customer is one car and one bad morning. A commercial account is nine vehicles, a maintenance schedule and an invoice that has to satisfy somebody's bookkeeper.

A commercial account is one contact and a garage full of vehicles.

The person who calls you usually isn't the person who drives the van. She runs the office, she keeps a spreadsheet with plate numbers in it, and what she wants is for six vehicles to be looked after without her having to think about any of them. Every question she asks is really the same question underneath: can I stop tracking this myself.

So the account gets built the way she already thinks about it. One customer record, several vehicles under it, each with its own mileage, its own history and its own next due date, and one contact who gets told what's happening before it happens.

  • Vehicles grouped under a business, not scattered across driver names
  • Mileage and service intervals recorded per vehicle
  • One contact for approvals, whoever ends up dropping the keys
  • Invoices addressed to the company, with a reference number if they need one

Contractor trucks and church vans keep somebody else's calendar.

A landscaping trailer can't be off the road in June. A van that carries a congregation on Sunday can sit in your bay all day Tuesday and nobody notices it's gone. Commercial scheduling is mostly knowing which of those you're holding, then booking the work into the week the customer can actually give up.

Online booking that offers real slots, with a reminder before the appointment, means she schedules at nine at night while she's doing the rest of her admin — instead of at ten in the morning while you're already under a hood with your hands full.

The invoice goes to a business, and the business has rules.

Retail customers pay at the counter and drive off. Companies want an invoice with the vehicle on it, terms they recognize and a way to pay that doesn't involve coming back. Some want it monthly, all of them want it consistent, and any shop that has chased a commercial invoice knows the chasing cost more than the discount ever did.

Invoices go out the same day the vehicle leaves, payment happens online or at the counter into one ledger, and what's outstanding becomes a screen instead of a feeling. It feeds the monthly report and syncs to QuickBooks, so the year-end takes an afternoon rather than a weekend.

Service history has to outlive the driver.

Drivers change. The van doesn't. Two years in, somebody new asks whether the timing belt has been done, and the honest answer is sitting in a paper folder that may or may not still be under the counter. A shop that can pull up every job against that plate in four seconds is a shop that keeps the account.

It's also where the next job comes from. A record showing the brake fluid was last changed at forty-one thousand miles is a phone call worth making at sixty-five thousand, and it's the rare call a customer thanks you for.

The call you missed was the one worth the most.

A worried driver will call three shops. A fleet contact calls one, and if nobody picks up she moves down her list and doesn't come back — because what she's buying isn't a repair, it's not having to think about it.

For $100 a month every call gets answered, at nine at night or on a Sunday morning, with what was said and the number to ring back waiting for you before you lock up.

Wheaton

Wheaton is the county seat, so DuPage County's offices at 421 N. County Farm Rd and the 18th Circuit courthouse at 505 N. County Farm Rd sit within a few miles of most bays in town. A place of 53,970 with the county's business running through it puts a lot of weekday miles on vehicles that belong to an organization rather than a household, and County Farm, Geneva, Naperville and Butterfield Roads carry them. Those drivers don't panic-search at seven in the morning. Somebody picks a shop once, writes it on a list, and keeps using it until the paperwork gets irritating.

Questions people actually ask

Straight answers.

Can you invoice a company rather than the driver?

Yes. The account belongs to the business, the vehicles sit under it, and the invoice goes to the contact who approves the work with the plate and the job on it. They can pay online or at the counter, and either way it lands in the same ledger.

Will the service history stay with the vehicle when the driver changes?

That's the point of recording it against the vehicle instead of the person. New driver, same plate, same history — every job, date and mileage still there when somebody asks what was done and when.
