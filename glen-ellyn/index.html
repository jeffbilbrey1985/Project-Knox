Glen Ellyn recognizes three business districts, and they don't behave alike.

The village names three: the historic downtown, the Roosevelt Road corridor, and Stacy's Corners at Main and Geneva — the Five Corners, if you've lived here a while. One village, one ZIP code, three completely different ways a customer ends up at your door.

Downtown is over a hundred shops in roughly 265,000 square feet, wrapped around the Metra station at 551 Crescent Blvd. Somebody there is already on the sidewalk with an hour to spend, so your website's job is smaller than most owners assume — confirm a decision that's half made, with hours that are right and a photo of the actual room instead of a stock interior.

Roosevelt Road is a different animal. Around 44,000 vehicles a day move along roughly 715,000 square feet of retail and service space, and the village's own corridor plan calls it pedestrian-, bicycle-, and automobile-unfriendly. Nobody's strolling past your sign out there. You're found on a phone or you're not found.

Most owners optimize hard for the district they're standing in and never think about the other two. What's below is built to cover all three at once.

We're based here, and here's what that actually buys you.

Project Knox works out of Glen Ellyn. On its own that's not a credential — plenty of good people build websites three states away, and for most of this work the distance stopped mattering years ago.

What it means in practice is that we're not guessing. We know downtown and Roosevelt Road are two different businesses inside one village. We know which corner somebody means when they give directions by landmark instead of address. And when you say your customers come off the train, we don't have to look up which train — it's the Union Pacific West line, 22.4 miles out from Ogilvie, and that shapes who's on your block at the end of a weekday and who isn't there at ten in the morning.

There's a plainer version of all this. When something breaks at nine at night, you're calling a number in your own area code, and whoever picks up has driven past your shop.

Glen Ellyn

The year here has a shape. Glenbard West stands above Lake Ellyn at 670 Crescent Blvd — a 1923 building people describe as a castle — and its calendar sets the rhythm of a lot of weekday afternoons. College of DuPage adds more than 20,000 students over on Fawell Blvd. August brings Taste of Glen Ellyn from the Chamber of Commerce. A village of 28,846 doesn't run at one steady speed across twelve months, and the shops that do well here book, staff and follow up differently in September than they do in February.
